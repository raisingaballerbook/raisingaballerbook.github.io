Building a shared language for sports parenting

The world of youth sports is full of buzzwords: development, mindset, balance, grit. Behind each one is something meaningful. Here are the key ideas from Raising a Baller, so parents, coaches, and athletes can speak the same language.

Sports can teach incredible lessons, but it helps when everyone speaks the same language. These concepts aren't just theory. They're tools to help young athletes thrive on and off the field.

Long-Term Athlete Development (LTAD)

A framework that focuses on skill progression, enjoyment, and long-term growth, not early specialization. LTAD helps parents understand what's right for their child's stage of development, so athletes build confidence, not burnout.

Growth mindset

The belief that effort and perseverance lead to improvement, and that talent isn't fixed. Teaching kids to see mistakes as part of learning gives them ownership of their progress and resilience when things get tough.

Physical literacy

The foundation of movement, coordination, and confidence that allows kids to play a variety of sports. Developing broad movement skills early builds athletic intelligence and a lifelong love of being active.

Emotional resilience

More than toughness: it's the ability to recover, adapt, and stay grounded. Helping young athletes process frustration and celebrate effort turns challenges into stepping stones.

The role of parents

Support doesn't mean control. Kids thrive when parents show consistency, empathy, and belief, without letting results define success. The best thing you can do is help them love the process.
